Add to Weeshlist From Any App: The Safari Extension, Explained

Weeshlist TeamJune 26, 20264 min read

The single biggest reason a wish never gets saved isn't forgetfulness — it's friction. You're already three taps deep in a shopping app, and switching to Weeshlist, finding the share sheet, and pasting the link is just enough extra effort to lose the moment. The Safari extension exists to remove that friction entirely.

A button that lives where you're already shopping

Once enabled, the Weeshlist extension adds a button directly on product pages in Safari. No switching apps, no copying a URL, no opening a share sheet — you tap the button on the page you're already looking at, and the item is saved.

Behind the scenes, the extension reads the same product data Weeshlist would extract from a pasted link — name, price, photo — and hands it straight to the app through a shared queue, so it's waiting for you the next time you open Weeshlist, even if you never left Safari.

The best version of a feature like this is the one you forget is even running — it's just a button on the page.

Turning it on

It's a one-time setup: open Safari's Settings, go to Extensions, enable Weeshlist, and allow it on the sites you shop from most. After that, the button appears automatically on product pages across those sites — no need to repeat the setup per store.

The extension only activates when you tap it. It doesn't run in the background, track pages you haven't chosen to save, or read anything beyond the page you're actively viewing at the moment you tap.

Built after watching where wishes actually get lost

This wasn't the first feature shipped — it came after noticing how often "I'll add this later" turns into never. Removing the extra steps between wanting something and saving it was the direct fix, and it's the kind of small, unglamorous improvement that ends up mattering the most.

The short version

  1. The extension adds a Weeshlist button directly on product pages in Safari.
  2. Tapping it saves the page's product data without opening the app or copying a link.
  3. Enable it once in Safari Settings → Extensions, allow it on your shopping sites.
  4. It only reads a page when you tap the button — nothing runs in the background.

A wishlist app is only as good as how easily you actually use it. The Safari extension was built to make "later" unnecessary — the wish is already saved before you've closed the tab.

Extension availability and behavior may vary slightly by iOS and Safari version.
