The No-Duplicate-Gift Problem, Solved

Weeshlist TeamJuly 10, 20265 min read

It happens at nearly every birthday: two people show up with the same sweater. Not because anyone was careless, but because a wishlist that lives in someone's head — or across five different group chats — has no way of staying in sync with itself.

Why it keeps happening

A static wishlist, whether it's a scribbled note or a screenshot forwarded around a family group chat, is only accurate the moment it's written. The instant one person buys an item, everyone else's copy of that list is already out of date — and nobody's going to re-forward a corrected version to a dozen people.

The result is predictable: gifts cluster on the "obvious" items at the top of the list, while the rest go untouched, and duplicates happen far more often than anyone plans for.

A wishlist that doesn't update itself is really just a list of guesses with a timestamp.

What a live, shared list changes

Weeshlist's wishlists are shared with friends the moment they're created, and stay live — when someone marks an item as gifted, it updates instantly for everyone following that list. Nobody has to text the group to say "I got the shoes"; the list already says so.

Because it's read-only for friends, nobody sees exactly who bought what, or when — just that an item is taken. The surprise element of gifting stays intact, while the coordination problem disappears.

Following without spoiling the surprise

Following a friend's wishlist through their unique ID (UID) doesn't notify them who's looking or what's been marked — it just keeps the list itself honest. That's the balance a group of gift-givers actually needs: enough visibility to avoid overlap, and none of the visibility that would ruin the moment.
